## 1. GAP-01 — Safety Committee: role, duties and agenda

### Authoritative source findings

- ILO's *Guidelines for implementing the OSH provisions of the MLC, 2006* state that MLC Standard A4.3(2)(d) requires a safety committee and safety representatives on ships with five or more seafarers (guidelines paragraph 71).
- The ILO describes the committee's objective as shipowner and seafarers from all levels / departments working together to promote safety and health and address workplace problems (paragraph 72).
- ILO guidance describes cooperation in implementing the ship's OSH policy, representation of the whole crew, regular meetings, meetings after serious incidents, communication among representatives, and distribution of minutes (paragraphs 73–80).
- The MCA's COSWP committee guidance explains that the committee is a consultation forum chaired by the Master; it should meet regularly and after serious incidents. It also sets practical agenda/minutes controls: circulate the agenda and previous minutes in advance; first agenda item is previous minutes and follow-up; penultimate item is AOB; final item is next meeting date/time/place; record conclusions and action tracking; circulate agreed minutes and relevant extracts to company.
- MCA publication MIN 688 (M) Amendment 5 identifies the COSWP 2026 edition, issued 18 March 2026 and amendment notice published 25 September 2026. The earlier 2024 COSWP PDF inspected for the detailed agenda clauses is therefore not the latest edition; verify the same clauses against the 2026 edition before using as current UK-flag guidance.
- F3 T4 Q85 and T3 Q21 already provide related safety-officer / monthly meeting material, but do not replace the ILO/COSWP committee framework.

### Proposed learning-unit structure (source-led synthesis, not a mandatory universal agenda)

1. Previous minutes, corrections and outstanding actions.
2. Accidents, incidents, near misses and hazardous conditions since last meeting.
3. Safety inspections, risk assessments, unsafe acts / conditions and corrective actions.
4. PPE, machinery, tools, permits, enclosed spaces, mooring / deck / engine-room work hazards.
5. Safety training, familiarisation, drills and safety communications.
6. Welfare / fatigue / occupational health issues raised by crew.
7. Crew / departmental representatives' recommendations and unresolved matters.
8. Any other business (AOB).
9. Next meeting date, time and place.

**Status:** Gap can now be drafted from authoritative source material, but flag-state / company-specific requirements and current COSWP 2026 text still need a final check. The agenda above is a structured synthesis of guidance, not a prescribed universal statutory agenda.

## 2. GAP-02 — Polar Code Chapter 9 navigation equipment and existing-ship provisions

### Original Polar Code — MSC.385(94), Chapter 9.3

Official IMO resolution text confirms:
- 9.3.1: means to receive and display current ice-condition information.
- 9.3.2.1.1: two independent echo-sounding devices, or one with two independent transducers, required for ice-strengthened ships constructed on or after 1 January 2017.
- 9.3.2.1.2: SOLAS V/22.1.9.4 clear-view requirements apply irrespective of construction date and size; where impossible, Administration-acceptable equivalent visibility arrangements.
- 9.3.2.1.3: prevent ice accumulation on navigation / communication antennas where ice accretion is likely.
- 9.3.2.1.4.1: protect projecting-under-hull sensors against ice on ice-strengthened ships.
- 9.3.2.1.4.2: enclosed / protected bridge wings apply to Category A and B ships constructed on or after 1 January 2017.
- 9.3.2.2: two independent non-magnetic heading means connected to main and emergency power; ships proceeding above 80° latitude require at least one GNSS compass or equivalent.
- 9.3.3: means to visually detect ice in darkness (subject to the 24-hour daylight exception); ships under icebreaker escort require a manually initiated flashing red light visible astern to indicate stopped.

### What “existing-ship exemption” actually means in the inspected text

Do not describe the whole Chapter 9 as waived for existing ships. The original text has construction-date thresholds for particular equipment/design requirements:
- The dual echo-sounder requirement in 9.3.2.1.1 is explicitly tied to ships constructed on or after 1 January 2017.
- The enclosed / protected bridge-wing requirement in 9.3.2.1.4.2 is explicitly tied to Category A/B ships constructed on or after 1 January 2017.
- By contrast, the clear-view-astern provision is expressly irrespective of construction date and size; other Chapter 9 provisions are not globally waived merely because a ship is existing.
- The 2017 entry-into-force arrangement for existing SOLAS ships was that Part I-A safety requirements applied by the first intermediate or renewal survey, whichever occurred first, after 1 January 2018; Part II-A pollution requirements applied to new and existing ships from 1 January 2017. Cite the IMO's 2017 entry-into-force explainer and check the ship's survey history / flag implementation.

### New amendments effective 1 January 2026 — MSC.538(107)

- MSC.538(107) separates Chapter 9 for SOLAS Chapter I-certified ships and introduces new Chapter 9-1 for fishing vessels 24 m and above, non-commercial pleasure yachts 300 GT and above, and cargo ships 300 GT to below 500 GT.
- New Chapter 9-1 includes ice-information reception/display, equipment functionality for expected environmental conditions, suitable heading / position-fixing systems, visual ice detection, clear view astern, ice-protected antennas, and certain construction-date-dependent echo-sounder / bridge-wing provisions.
- The new provisions apply from 1 January 2026. The UK implementing regulations provide a useful national example: qualifying additional-category ships constructed before 1 January 2026 are allowed until 1 January 2027 to comply with Chapters 9-1 and 11-1; new ships comply from entry into force. This is a transitional date for the newly covered non-SOLAS categories, not a blanket exemption for all existing ships and not a universal statement of every flag's domestic enforcement mechanism.
- The January 2026 Polar Code supplement reproduces the official amendments and is the best working source for the current text.

**Status:** Gap substantially closed at source-research level. Draft the answer in two distinct parts: (A) Chapter 9 equipment requirements and (B) construction-date / existing-ship transition provisions. Technical review must confirm vessel category, construction date, operating latitude, ice class, and current Administration implementation.

## 3. GAP-03 — Chemical tanker Type 1 / Type 2 cargo-space fire contingency plan

### Authoritative source findings

- IMO confirms chemical carriage in bulk is governed by SOLAS Chapter VII and MARPOL Annex II, and chemical tankers built after 1 July 1986 are required to comply with the IBC Code.
- IMO defines Type 1 ships as carrying products with very severe environmental and safety hazards requiring maximum preventive measures; Type 2 ships carry products with appreciably severe hazards requiring significant preventive measures. The IBC Code Chapter 17 product entry determines ship type and product-specific carriage requirements.
- The IBC Code contains fire-protection / fire-extinction requirements (Chapter 11 in the 2004 revised text; verify the current edition / amendments before citing exact paragraph numbers). FSS Code and SOLAS II-2 requirements also form part of the ship's fire-safety framework, subject to IBC Code provisions and approved ship arrangements.
- BWK E14 covers oil / NLS spillage, not the full cargo-space fire plan. F3 fixed fire-fighting system and tanker-deck explosion answers are only equipment / scenario support; they are not an approved cargo-space fire plan.

### Safe drafting boundary

Do not invent one generic Type 1/Type 2 fire-fighting release procedure. Before a learning unit is drafted, establish:
1. Cargo identity and IBC Code Chapter 17 entry / hazards / fire-fighting media compatibility.
2. Ship type, cargo containment, cargo-space arrangement, fixed system design and approved fire-control plan.
3. Applicable SOLAS II-2, FSS Code and IBC Code requirements in the current consolidated editions.
4. Ship-specific SMS emergency checklist, cargo-specific SDS / emergency response information, and Master's / shore response interfaces.
5. Whether the scenario is cargo tank fire, cargo-space fire, deck/manifold fire, pump-room fire, or external fire threatening the cargo area; these are not interchangeable.
6. Toxicity, flammability, water-reactivity / incompatibility, vapour behaviour, exposure controls, boundary cooling and environmental response as applicable to the specific product and ship arrangement.

**Status:** Still open for answer drafting. Official framework identified, but exact current IBC / FSS text and cargo-specific approved procedures must be obtained and reviewed by a qualified chemical-tanker Master / Chief Officer and fire-safety specialist. Do not publish an operational response checklist based only on generic notes.
