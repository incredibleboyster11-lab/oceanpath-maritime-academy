# OceanPath Regulatory Gap Research — Batch 21
## Chemical tanker Type 1 / Type 2 cargo-space fire and NLS spillage

**Date:** 15 November 2026  
**Status:** Internal source research; not an approved shipboard procedure or public learning unit.  
**Source prompt:** BWK Nov 2022 Q2 asks for a contingency plan for a fire incident in cargo spaces of a chemical tanker (IMO Type 1 & 2) and spillage of NLS. The user's BWK source marks the fire-plan answer as not yet covered; its NLS spill section exists separately.

## 1. Critical interpretation of the question

- IMO Type 1 and Type 2 are **chemical-tanker ship design categories** determined by the hazards of the products listed in IBC Code Chapter 17. They are not fire classes and do not, by themselves, identify the correct extinguishing medium.
- Type 1 ships are intended for products with very severe environmental and safety hazards and require maximum preventive measures; Type 2 ships carry products with appreciably severe hazards requiring significant preventive measures.
- The actual fire response depends on the product's physical/chemical properties, IBC Code Chapter 17 product entry, column-l fire-protection equipment / media, ship's approved arrangement, cargo location and whether the event is a tank fire, deck/manifold fire, cargo machinery-space fire or fire threatening the cargo area.
- Therefore a generic statement such as “use foam” or “use water” for every Type 1 / Type 2 cargo-space fire is unsafe and technically unsound.

## 2. Applicable regulatory framework

### 2.1 SOLAS / IBC Code relationship

- SOLAS Chapter VII Part B and MARPOL Annex II govern carriage of dangerous liquid chemicals / NLS in bulk.
- Chemical tankers built after 1 July 1986 are generally required to comply with the IBC Code; earlier ships fall under the applicable BCH Code regime.
- The IBC Code Chapter 11 applies tanker fire-protection requirements to ships covered by the Code, subject to the specific provisions / exceptions in 11.1.
- IBC Code 11.3 requires a fixed deck foam system. The foam concentrate must be effective for the maximum possible number of cargoes; additional arrangements must be provided for cargoes for which foam is ineffective or incompatible. Regular protein foam is not to be used. The arrangement must cover the cargo tank deck area and be capable of delivering foam into a cargo tank whose deck is assumed ruptured.
- IBC Code 11.4 requires fixed dry-chemical powder systems for ships intended to carry flammable products, for deck cargo-area and cargo liquid/vapour connections. This is a deck protection system; do not misdescribe it as a universal tank-interior extinguishing method.
- IBC Code 11.5 addresses enclosed spaces containing cargo-handling equipment / cargo machinery rooms and requires an appropriate fixed system under the FSS Code, taking account of the concentration/application rate needed for the cargo hazard.
- IBC Code 16.2.3 requires cargo information to be on board and available to concerned personnel. It includes cargo properties/reactivity, spill/leak action, personal-contact countermeasures, fire-fighting procedures and media, cargo transfer / tank cleaning / gas-freeing / ballasting procedures.
- IBC Code 16.3.3 requires officers to be trained in emergency procedures for leakage, spillage or fire involving cargoes, and enough officers trained in relevant first aid, based on IMO guidance.
- SOLAS II-2 Regulation 16 requires fire-safety operational booklets. For ships carrying dangerous goods / flammable bulk cargoes, the booklet is to refer to relevant IBC Code and emergency cargo-handling instructions. Tanker fire-safety operations include controlling flammable vapour releases, cargo tank purging / gas-freeing and inert-gas operation as applicable.
- SOLAS II-2 Regulation 10 and the FSS Code govern ship fire-fighting systems. The ship's fire-control plan, approved fixed installations, cargo compatibility and emergency plan remain controlling.

### 2.2 Cargo-specific selection of fire-fighting media

- The IBC Code Chapter 17 product entry and associated fire-protection information must be consulted for the actual cargo.
- The IBC Code identifies appropriate media according to product properties. Examples in the source include alcohol-resistant foam and/or regular foam according to solubility, water spray generally as a coolant where water-reactivity index permits, and dry chemical where water-reactivity requires it.
- These are not blanket instructions. The specific cargo entry, ship's approved fire plan and safety data must govern.
- Do not apply water or foam to a water-reactive cargo unless the approved cargo-specific instructions permit it. Do not assume foam is effective for every polar solvent or chemical cargo.

## 3. Shipboard contingency-plan architecture — educational structure only

A safe exam-learning structure can be organised under the ship's approved SMS / emergency plan, but must not prescribe unverified tactics or extinguishing-media release.

### A. Initial command actions
1. Raise the alarm and report the exact location / nature of the fire; inform the Master and activate the appropriate fire emergency organisation.
2. Stop cargo transfer and related operations when safe to do so; isolate transfer lines / pumps / valves using the approved cargo emergency procedures.
3. Muster personnel, account for crew and contractors, establish exclusion zones and keep non-essential personnel clear.
4. Identify the cargo and obtain the cargo-specific IBC Chapter 17 entry, SDS, fire-fighting media and toxicity / reactivity hazards.
5. Inform engine room, terminal / port control and Company; prepare external assistance / fire brigade interface as required by the SMS and port plan.
6. Consider immediate danger to life, escalation, vapour cloud, toxic exposure, loss of containment and risk to adjacent tanks / spaces.

### B. Emergency / fire party
1. Approach only under the ship's approved fire plan and command authorisation, with correct fire-fighter's outfits and SCBA.
2. Confirm safe access, escape route, communications, wind direction, atmosphere and exposure risk before entry / attack.
3. Select fire-fighting medium only after verifying cargo-specific compatibility and approved system design.
4. Protect adjacent tanks / boundaries and exposed equipment using approved arrangements; monitor temperature, pressure, vapour and structural condition.
5. Do not enter cargo tanks or enclosed spaces for fire-fighting / rescue without the enclosed-space and breathing-apparatus procedures, rescue standby and permit / command controls required by SMS.
6. Prepare for personnel decontamination / first aid and medical advice using MFAG / cargo SDS where applicable.

### C. Engine / technical team
1. Maintain readiness of fire pumps, emergency fire pump, power supply and approved fixed systems.
2. Isolate ventilation, electrical sources, cargo pumps and machinery as directed by the approved plan, while preserving essential services.
3. Operate fixed systems only under the ship's approved release procedure, after personnel evacuation / headcount and all required pre-release checks.
4. Monitor stability, stress, tank pressure, inert-gas condition and risk of escalation; record key readings.
5. Avoid any improvised release of CO2, foam, water or dry powder into a cargo space.

### D. Support / medical / communications
1. Account for all persons and establish a safe muster / triage area.
2. Treat chemical exposure using cargo SDS / MFAG and arrange medical advice.
3. Prepare rescue and evacuation arrangements; keep escape routes clear.
4. Record time, location, cargo, quantity involved, actions and communications; preserve records / VDR as directed.
5. Notify Company, terminal, port / coastal authorities and emergency services in accordance with SMS, statutory reporting and local arrangements.

### E. Subsequent response
1. Confirm fire extinguished and atmosphere / re-ignition risk assessed by competent personnel.
2. Maintain boundary / atmosphere monitoring and re-entry controls.
3. Assess cargo tank / structural damage, adjacent cargoes, pollution and stability.
4. Arrange cargo transfer / discharge / tank cleaning / gas-freeing only under approved cargo-specific procedures and competent supervision.
5. Complete incident reports, evidence preservation, investigation, corrective actions and controlled return to operation.

**This is an educational organisation of response roles, not a substitute for a vessel's approved fire-control plan, SMS checklist, cargo-specific procedures or Master's judgement.**

## 4. NLS spillage — keep as a separate but linked response plan

The existing BWK E14 answer covers spill response. It should remain a distinct branch because the first priority and hazards differ from a fire:
- Stop the source / cargo transfer and raise the alarm.
- Identify cargo, category, quantity, location, exposure and water-reactivity / vapour hazards.
- Activate the ship's SMPEP / applicable pollution plan and notify the Master, Company, terminal and relevant authorities as required.
- Protect personnel first; isolate area and use cargo-specific PPE / SCBA as required by SDS / MFAG.
- Prevent entry into sea / drains using approved scuppers, save-alls, drip trays and compatible containment materials, only where safe and chemically compatible.
- Do not use absorbents, water, dispersants or neutralising agents unless compatible with the substance and permitted by the approved plan / authority.
- Contain and recover only by trained personnel using cargo-specific arrangements; monitor vapour / toxicity and prevent ignition.
- Record estimates, samples / photographs where safe, position, time, weather, notifications and response.
- Arrange disposal / tank cleaning / recovery under MARPOL Annex II, ship's approved procedures and port requirements.

The existing E14 material includes generic spill actions such as stopping operations, alarm, scupper plugging, PPE, fire-fighting readiness and notifications. For chemical cargoes, replace generic “oil deck drain / absorbent / dispersant” language with cargo-specific compatibility checks; an oil-spill technique is not automatically suitable for NLS.

## 5. Open technical questions before an exam-ready final answer

1. Obtain the current controlled IBC Code edition and verify the exact Chapter 11 text and amendments in force.
2. Confirm the product-specific column-l fire-protection equipment and media for representative Type 1 and Type 2 cargoes.
3. Establish whether the exam wording “cargo spaces” means cargo tanks, cargo pump-room / cargo machinery space, or the wider cargo area. The answer should state the assumed scope.
4. Confirm whether the examiner expects the IBC Code fixed systems, a general shipboard contingency plan, or both.
5. Obtain qualified chemical-tanker Master / Chief Officer and fire-safety specialist review.
6. Keep the Type 1 / Type 2 distinction accurate: ship design category, not fire class.

## 7. Research conclusion

- The gap is now supported by a reliable regulatory framework and an educational response architecture.
- A universal operational fire-fighting procedure is **not** approved or claimed.
- The key technical teaching point is that Type 1 / Type 2 describe ship design standards; actual fire media and actions depend on the cargo and approved ship arrangements.
- The final exam answer may be prepared after specialist review, but any operational detail must remain subordinate to the cargo-specific IBC entry, SDS, approved fire plan and SMS.
