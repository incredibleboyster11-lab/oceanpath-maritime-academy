# Answer 1 — Role and duties of the Safety Committee on board; agenda of a meeting

## 1. Purpose

The Safety Committee is a shipboard forum where the Master, safety officials and seafarer representatives from different departments work together to promote occupational safety and health, review unsafe conditions and improve the ship's safety culture.

Under the MLC, 2006, a Safety Committee and safety representatives are required on board ships with five or more seafarers (Standard A4.3, paragraph 2(d)).

## 2. Composition

- Master — Chairperson.
- Safety Officer / person designated by the Master.
- Elected or appointed safety representatives.
- Representatives should, as far as practicable, cover the different departments and working groups, such as deck, engine and catering.
- The safety officer, where appointed, should be a member.

## 3. Main roles and duties

1. Cooperate with the Master and company in implementing the ship's occupational safety and health policy.
2. Review unsafe conditions, hazardous situations and risks affecting crew.
3. Discuss accidents, incidents, near misses and lessons learned.
4. Review risk assessments, safe working procedures and permit-to-work arrangements.
5. Recommend preventive and corrective measures to avoid recurrence of accidents.
6. Promote proper use of PPE, safe equipment and good housekeeping.
7. Encourage crew participation, reporting of hazards and a positive safety culture.
8. Review safety inspections, outstanding defects and action items.
9. Identify safety training and familiarisation needs.
10. Communicate recommendations to the Master / company and monitor the action taken.

## 4. Suggested agenda of a Safety Committee meeting

*The following is a practical agenda synthesised from ILO and MCA guidance. It is not presented as a universal statutory agenda.*

1. Opening of meeting and attendance.
2. Confirmation of previous meeting minutes.
3. Review of outstanding actions and company responses.
4. Accidents, incidents, near misses and hazardous occurrences.
5. Safety inspections and unsafe conditions.
6. Risk assessments, permits to work and safe working procedures.
7. PPE, tools, machinery, mooring, enclosed-space and other work-related hazards.
8. Safety drills, training, familiarisation and safety communications.
9. Crew suggestions, departmental concerns and unresolved safety issues.
10. Any Other Business (AOB).
11. Date, time and place of the next meeting.

## 5. Records and follow-up

- Record the matters discussed, decisions and recommendations in the minutes.
- Assign responsibility and target dates for corrective actions.
- Circulate the minutes to committee members and make relevant information available to the crew.
- Send relevant extracts to the company through the Master and record the company's response.
- Convene a meeting after a serious accident or incident when required by the applicable arrangements.

**Conclusion:** The Safety Committee promotes cooperation between the Master, company and crew. Its main purpose is prevention of accidents and continuous improvement of safety on board.

**Source references:** MLC 2006, Standard A4.3(2)(d); ILO *Guidelines for implementing the OSH provisions of the MLC, 2006*, Chapter 5; applicable flag-state legislation and current company SMS / safety procedures. MCA COSWP is UK-flag guidance and must not automatically be treated as the governing law for every flag.

# Answer 2 — Polar Code: navigation-system requirements and provisions for existing ships

## 1. Introduction

The International Code for Ships Operating in Polar Waters (Polar Code) is mandatory under SOLAS Chapter XIV and relevant MARPOL Annexes. It addresses the special hazards of Arctic and Antarctic navigation, including ice, extreme cold, limited hydrographic information and remoteness from search and rescue facilities.

The Polar Code's navigation requirements are contained in Part I-A, Chapter 9 for ships certified in accordance with SOLAS Chapter I. From 1 January 2026, new Chapter 9-1 applies to specified non-SOLAS categories.

## 2. Main navigation-system requirements — Chapter 9

### A. Nautical information
- The ship must have means to receive and display current information on ice conditions in the operating area.
- Navigational information must be suitable for the intended polar voyage.

### B. Navigational equipment functionality
- Navigational equipment and systems must remain functional under the expected environmental conditions.
- Heading-reference and position-fixing systems must be suitable for the intended operating area.
- Equipment sensors projecting below the hull must be protected against ice where required.
- Means must be provided to prevent ice accumulation on navigation and communication antennas where ice accretion is likely.
- The ship must have a clear view astern in accordance with SOLAS V/22.1.9.4; where direct compliance is not possible, an Administration-accepted equivalent arrangement is required.

### C. Echo-sounding equipment
- Ice-strengthened ships constructed on or after 1 January 2017 must have either:
  - two independent echo-sounding devices; or
  - one echo-sounding device with two separate independent transducers.

### D. Heading and position-fixing equipment
- Two independent non-magnetic means must be provided to determine and display heading.
- Both must be connected to the ship's main and emergency sources of power.
- Ships proceeding to latitudes above 80° must have at least one GNSS compass or equivalent, connected to main and emergency power.

### E. Visual detection of ice
- Ships must have means to visually detect ice when operating in darkness.
- The original Chapter 9 requires two remotely rotatable narrow-beam searchlights controllable from the bridge, providing 360° lighting, or other means to visually detect ice.
- Ships under icebreaker escort must have a manually initiated flashing red light visible astern to indicate when the ship is stopped.

## 3. Existing ships — construction-date provisions

Do not state that existing ships are generally exempt from the whole Polar Code.

Under original Chapter 9.3, two equipment/design requirements have explicit construction-date thresholds:

1. The dual echo-sounder requirement in paragraph 9.3.2.1.1 applies to ice-strengthened ships constructed on or after 1 January 2017.
2. The enclosed / protected bridge-wing requirement in paragraph 9.3.2.1.4.2 applies to Category A and B ships constructed on or after 1 January 2017.

Other requirements must not be assumed exempt merely because the ship was built before 2017. For example, the clear-view-astern requirement is expressly stated to apply irrespective of construction date and size. Check each paragraph separately.

For SOLAS ships constructed before 1 January 2017, the original Polar Code safety provisions were to be met by the first intermediate or renewal survey, whichever occurred first, after 1 January 2018. Pollution-prevention provisions applied to existing and new ships from 1 January 2017.

## 4. New provisions effective 1 January 2026

IMO Resolution MSC.538(107) amended the Polar Code and inserted Chapter 9-1 for:
- fishing vessels of 24 m length overall and above;
- pleasure yachts of 300 GT and above not engaged in trade; and
- cargo ships of 300 GT and above but below 500 GT.

Chapter 9-1 sets navigation requirements for these categories. Under the UK implementing regulations, qualifying ships in these categories constructed before 1 January 2026 have until 1 January 2027 to comply with the new Chapters 9-1 and 11-1. This is a jurisdiction-specific implementation example; verify the applicable flag Administration's implementation before relying on the date.

## 5. Conclusion

The Polar Code requires reliable ice information, equipment suitable for extreme environmental conditions, independent heading means, suitable position fixing, ice detection and appropriate visibility. Existing-ship provisions are specific construction-date rules, not a blanket exemption. The ship's category, construction date, ice class, operating latitude and Administration requirements must be checked before a Polar voyage.

**Source references:** Polar Code 2016 Edition, Chapter 9.3; IMO Resolution MSC.385(94); IMO Resolution MSC.538(107), effective 1 January 2026; January 2026 Polar Code Supplement; SOLAS Chapter XIV. Confirm the current consolidated Code and flag-state implementation before use.
