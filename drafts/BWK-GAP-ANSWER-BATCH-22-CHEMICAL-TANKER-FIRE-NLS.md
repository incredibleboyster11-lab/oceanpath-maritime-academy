# BWK Q2 — Chemical Tanker Cargo-Space Fire (IMO Type 1 & 2) and NLS Spillage
## Internal exam-answer draft — source-led, pending specialist review

**Question source:** BWK Nov 2022 Q2.  
**Marks:** 20.  
**Status:** Educational draft only. It is not a ship-specific emergency checklist and must not override the Master's orders, approved fire-control plan, cargo-specific instructions or SMS.

## 1. Introduction

Chemical tankers carrying dangerous liquid chemicals in bulk are subject to SOLAS Chapter VII, MARPOL Annex II and the IBC Code, where applicable. IMO Type 1 and Type 2 describe ship design standards based on the hazards of the products carried; they are not fire classes. The actual response and extinguishing medium depend on the cargo's IBC Code Chapter 17 entry, cargo properties, safety data and the ship's approved arrangements.

## 2. Fire in cargo area / cargo space of a chemical tanker

### A. Initial / command actions
1. Raise the alarm, inform the Master and announce the exact location and nature of the fire.
2. Stop cargo transfer and related operations when safe; isolate pumps, lines and valves as directed by the approved cargo emergency procedure.
3. Muster and account for all crew, terminal personnel and visitors; establish a safe exclusion zone.
4. Identify the cargo involved and consult the IBC Code Chapter 17 entry, cargo information, SDS and fire-fighting media compatibility.
5. Inform the Company, terminal / port control and emergency services in accordance with the ship's SMS and port emergency plan.
6. Assess risk of toxic vapour, flammable atmosphere, escalation, loss of containment and danger to adjacent tanks / spaces.

### B. Fire / emergency team
1. Approach only under the Master's command and approved fire plan, wearing suitable fire-fighter's outfit and SCBA.
2. Confirm safe access, escape route, communications, atmosphere and exposure hazards.
3. Use only the fire-fighting medium approved for the cargo and ship arrangement. Check IBC Code Chapter 17 fire-protection information and the ship's approved fire plan; do not assume water or foam is suitable for every chemical.
4. Protect adjacent tanks, boundaries and exposed equipment using the approved system and cargo-compatible medium.
5. Prevent unauthorised entry into cargo tanks or enclosed spaces; rescue and entry must follow enclosed-space / SCBA procedures and standby arrangements.
6. Prepare first aid, decontamination and medical assistance using cargo SDS / MFAG information.

### C. Engine / technical team
1. Keep fire pumps, emergency fire pump, power and approved fire-fighting systems ready.
2. Isolate ventilation, electrical sources and cargo machinery as required by the approved plan.
3. Operate a fixed system only in accordance with its approved release procedure, after evacuation, headcount and all prescribed safety checks.
4. Monitor tank pressure, inert-gas condition, stability, structural condition and the possibility of fire spread.
5. Do not improvise the release of CO2, foam, water or dry powder into a cargo space.

### D. Support team and subsequent response
1. Account for all persons and establish a safe muster / medical area.
2. Maintain communications with the terminal, Company and shore fire-fighting teams.
3. Record the time, position, cargo, incident details, actions and notifications; preserve records / VDR as directed.
4. After extinguishment, maintain boundary and atmosphere monitoring; control re-entry and re-ignition risks.
5. Assess damage, cargo condition, pollution and stability. Resume cargo operations only after the Master / Company / competent authorities determine it is safe.

## 3. Regulatory fire-protection points

- IBC Code Chapter 11.3: fixed deck foam system; concentrate must be suitable for the cargoes carried, with additional arrangements for cargoes for which foam is ineffective or incompatible.
- IBC Code Chapter 11.4: fixed dry-chemical powder system for the exposed cargo deck / cargo liquid and vapour connections when flammable products are carried. This is not a universal tank-interior extinguishing method.
- IBC Code Chapter 11.5: fixed fire-extinguishing arrangements for enclosed spaces containing cargo-handling equipment / cargo machinery rooms, as applicable.
- IBC Code 16.2.3: cargo information available on board must include fire-fighting procedures and media.
- IBC Code 16.3.3: officers are to be trained in emergency procedures for leakage, spillage or fire involving cargoes.
- SOLAS II-2 Regulation 16: fire-safety operational booklet includes cargo-operation fire-safety instructions and references to the IBC Code, as appropriate.

## 4. Noxious Liquid Substance (NLS) spillage

### A. Immediate actions
1. Stop cargo transfer / source of leakage when safe; raise the alarm and inform the Master.
2. Identify the substance, quantity, location, hazards and whether it is toxic, corrosive, flammable or water-reactive.
3. Activate the ship's approved SMPEP / pollution response plan and notify the Company, terminal and authorities as required.
4. Isolate the area, protect personnel and use PPE / SCBA according to the cargo SDS and risk assessment.
5. Prevent the substance from entering the sea or drains using compatible containment measures, save-alls and scupper protection where safe.
6. Keep ignition sources controlled where the cargo is flammable; maintain fire-fighting readiness.

### B. Containment and recovery
1. Use only cargo-compatible absorbents, transfer equipment and containment materials.
2. Do not apply water, dispersants, neutralising agents or generic oil-spill materials unless compatibility is confirmed by cargo information and the approved plan.
3. Recover / transfer the substance to a suitable tank or approved receptacle under competent supervision.
4. Provide first aid and medical assistance using SDS / MFAG guidance.
5. Record the position, time, estimated quantity, weather, substance, actions and notifications; retain samples / photographs where safe and required.

### C. Subsequent response
1. Inform the Company and relevant coastal / port authorities in accordance with the approved plan and statutory reporting requirements.
2. Arrange safe disposal, tank cleaning and restoration under MARPOL Annex II, the IBC Code and port requirements.
3. Investigate the cause, preserve evidence and implement corrective actions.
4. Resume operations only after the Master and responsible authorities / terminal confirm it is safe.

## 5. Conclusion

The Master coordinates the response, protects life first, controls the source, identifies the cargo hazard and uses only approved cargo-compatible fire-fighting or spill-response arrangements. Type 1 / Type 2 ship classification does not determine a universal fire medium. The IBC Code cargo entry, SDS, approved fire-control plan and SMS govern the actual response.

**References:** SOLAS Chapter VII; SOLAS Chapter II-2 Regulations 10 and 16; MARPOL Annex II; IBC Code Chapters 11, 16 and 17; ship's approved fire-control plan, cargo information / SDS and SMS emergency procedures; MFAG where applicable.
