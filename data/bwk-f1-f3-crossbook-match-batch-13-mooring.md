## 3. Source-derived observations and review flags

1. **Running vs standing moor:** BWK, F1 and F3 all treat these as two-anchor methods for restricted swinging room / reversing tidal stream. The key distinction is whether the ship uses headway to lay out the anchors (running moor) or stems the tide and drops astern with the current (standing moor).
2. **Selection context:** F1 T7 Q71 explicitly identifies running moor as the usual answer when current is from behind, while F3 T7 Q7 describes running moor with tide from astern or stemming the tide with headway. Preserve both phrasings and check the exact operational scenario before choosing the method.
3. **Anchor order / labels:** Source passages use “upstream”, “downstream”, “weather”, “lee”, “first” and “second” anchors in different contexts. Do not harmonise these labels without mapping the current direction and final position in the source diagram.
4. **Numerical cable lengths:** BWK M1/M2 includes illustrative shackles and scope values. F1/F3 often use “about twice the required scope” as a general sequence. Do not treat a single example length as universal; vessel, depth, holding ground, current, weather and anchor plan govern.
5. **Mooring between buoys:** BWK M3 is a distinct 20-mark manoeuvring scenario. The inspected F1/F3 material covers river-port mooring and general mooring plans, not the full buoy-to-buoy procedure.
6. **Current from astern:** BWK M3 uses the head line as a pivot and lets the stream carry the stern round; this is not interchangeable with the two-anchor running/standing moor.
7. **Safety:** BWK M3 stresses lookout, mooring boat competence, engines ready and line strain. Public learning derivatives should add no unverified procedural assumptions and require qualified review.
