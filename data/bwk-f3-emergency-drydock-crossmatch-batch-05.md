## 2. Additional distinctions

1. **Abandon ship versus abandon-ship drill:** BWK E10 is an operational decision and survival-craft procedure. F3 Q30/Q11 are training and drill requirements; F3 Q34 is a foundering scenario with abandon-ship preparation.
2. **Fire wallet versus fire control plan:** BWK E11 is an incident response and shore-brigade handover. F3 Q84 describes plan content and fire-control-station information. Related, not identical.
3. **CO2 operation versus testing:** BWK E12 and F3 Q38 both cover operational release precautions; F3 Q9/Q12/Q21 cover line tests, system requirements and maintenance.
4. **Flooding scenarios:** F3 Q29 is flooding through a hull hole; BWK E15 includes engine-room flooding and a separate blackout-in-TSS scenario. Preserve the different starting conditions.
5. **TSS steering failure is not blackout:** F3 Q110 covers steering-gear failure and the emergency steering response. Do not treat it as a power blackout answer.
6. **Dry-dock fatality:** F3 Q94 adds a specific death-at-work response, scene preservation, notification and investigation that is not the same as BWK S5's general emergency taxonomy.

## 3. Implications for future learning units

- E12 can use a shared core CO2-release learning objective, with separate source-specific steps and a qualified technical review.
- E10 should have distinct operational abandonment, foundering scenario and drill-frequency sub-units.
- E11 should link fire-wallet / fire-control-plan content to port-fire response, but preserve the incident-command sequence as its own unit.
- E14 remains a source gap in the inspected F3 scope and should not be filled by adapting an unrelated engine-room or store-fire answer.
- E15 requires separate units for flooding and blackout; emergency-generator and steering-failure content can be linked as prerequisites, not substituted.
- S5 should link dry-dock stability hazards and fatality response while retaining BWK's broader safety checklist.
